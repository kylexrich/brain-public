# Sensitive Information

Anything that could reasonably cause significant reputational or security harm to the business if made public.

## Categories

1. **Security incidents** — credential leaks, API key exposures, secret rotations, vulnerability disclosures, unauthorized access, or any
   moment where a secret is visible on screen.

2. **Internal infrastructure** — database names, internal hostnames, API endpoints, architecture diagrams, service topology, or any detail
   that would help an attacker map the system.

3. **People's names and personal information** — names of individuals outside Kyle's immediate team or close friendships, personal contact
   details, job titles paired with names, or any information that could identify a private individual. This includes people like customers,
   investors, or private messages Kyle doesn't intend to expose.

4. **Customer and client information** — customer names, account details, usage data, contracts, or anything that could identify a specific
   customer or reveal the nature of that relationship.

5. **Business financials and metrics** — revenue figures, margins, runway, cost structures, growth rates, or any non-public financial data.

6. **Legal and compliance matters** — ongoing disputes, NDA-covered information, regulatory issues, or legal strategy.

7. **Vendor and partnership details** — contract terms, commercial arrangements, or relationships that are not publicly disclosed.

8. **Internal communications** — private Slack messages, emails, or conversations that were not intended for a public audience.

9. **Negative commentary about named individuals or companies** — criticism, frustration, or negative sentiment directed at a specific named
   person or organization, regardless of whether the underlying opinion is justified.
